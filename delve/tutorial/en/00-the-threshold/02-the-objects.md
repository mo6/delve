# Picking Things Up

The Peddler has a blanket spread on the floor with nothing on it, and the manner of someone
who would still like to sell you something.

"The Porter told you the screen. I'll tell you the pockets. There's a stone on this floor,
worth nothing, and I want you to carry it about a bit. Humour me."

**Stand on a thing and press `,` to pick it up.** The comma. That's the whole trick. When
you are standing on something you can take, the bottom line says so; walk onto the stone and
look down.

**Press `i` to see what you're carrying.** Your pack. The stone will be in it, and not much
else yet.

**Press `d` to put something down again.** Drop. You choose what, and how much, and it lands
on the floor where you stand.

"Coins are the one exception, and a happy one," says the Peddler. "You never pick money up.
Step onto it and it's already counted; the purse on your status line just grows. Everything
else waits politely for your comma."

> 🎒 `,` picks up. `i` looks in your pack. `d` puts down. Coins collect themselves; everything
> else you carry by choice.

"Carry the stone through my door if you like, or leave it on my blanket. It was never really
the point. The point was your fingers know three more keys than they did a minute ago."

## Questions

### A coin looks after itself when you walk over it, but the stone does not. How do you pick the stone up, and how would you set it down again?

- [x] Stand on it and press `,` to pick it up; press `d` to put it down
- [ ] Walk over it like a coin; press `i` to put it down
- [ ] Press `i` to pick it up; press `,` to put it down
- [ ] Kick it into your pack; press `d` only to open the pack

> Stand on it and press `,` to pick it up; press `d` to put something back down. `i` only opens
> your pack so you can see what you hold. Coins are the exception that proves it: they collect
> themselves the moment you step on them, so money never needs a key. Everything else waits for
> you to reach for it.
