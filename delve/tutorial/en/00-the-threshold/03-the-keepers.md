# How This Works

"You saw what happened," says Alwin. "You answered the Porter, and a door appeared in a
wall that had no door in it. That's the entire game. I'm only going to tell you what you
already watched."

**Every room has a keeper. Every keeper has a lesson and a question or two.** Walk up to
them, hear them out, answer. Pass, and a door appears in the stone and lets you onward.
Fail, and it doesn't.

There's no way around a keeper, not because anyone's watching, but because **the door
does not exist until you've earned it.** There's no path to sneak along, no corridor
someone forgot to lock. It isn't there yet.

**The doors you've earned stay open forever.** Go back and ask any keeper to teach you
again, as often as you like. It costs nothing. Re-reading is the whole point of this
place; you will never be punished for it.

"But hear the other half," says Alwin, "because it's the half people wish I'd left out."

**An examination is sat once.** The moment you pass a room, that score is final. I'll
happily explain my lesson to you nine more times; I'll never ask you the question again.
Otherwise anyone could grind a room to a perfect score, and the scroll at the bottom would
be worth nothing to anybody.

**Wrong answers cost HP.** Downstairs, they do. Get an answer wrong and you'll feel it,
and if your HP runs out you'll wake up back at the entrance to that floor, **with every
door you'd earned still open.** You don't lose progress here. Ever. The stakes exist to
make answers feel like they weigh something, not to take your afternoon away.

**The jackal will follow you down.** That's Algo. At any gate you can consult her instead
of answering: she'll nudge you toward the shape of it, and it costs you a point on that
room. Sometimes a nudge is exactly right and the point is a bargain. That's your call.

> 🚪 Doors appear when you pass. Doors stay open. You are never punished for going back.

"One more door," Alwin says, nodding at the far wall. "Merryn keeps the purse. After that the
stairs are yours. I don't know what the training below is about; I've been standing here since
before it was written."
