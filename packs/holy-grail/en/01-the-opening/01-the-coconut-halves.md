# Two empty halves of the coconut

Patsy knocks two coconut halves together and looks offended that you noticed.

"The king needs a horse," he says. "We have the sound of a horse. In this
economy, that is close enough for government work, until some guard at the
castle asks where you got a coconut in England."

He knocks again. "That question is the whole scene. Not 'is the king brave?'
Not 'is the quest holy?' Just: *where did the coconut come from?* And suddenly
everyone is doing migratory ornithology in a muddy courtyard, because the
simplest prop has a supply-chain problem."

"A swallow could have carried it, someone suggests. Then: African or European?
Then: could a swallow carry a coconut? Then: what about two swallows with a
strand of creeper? The argument escalates because nobody will say the quiet
part: we are making the horse noise with grocery items."

> Before you invent an elaborate theory, check whether the prop is allowed to
> be a prop.

"Your lesson is not about birds yet. That is the next room. This room is about
noticing when a story is papering over a coconut."
