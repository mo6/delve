# Bring out your dead!

The Collector rings a bell and does not look at faces. The cart is already
full. Someone tries to load an old man who is still complaining.

"I'm not dead," says the old man.

"He will be soon," says the relative, helpfully.

The Collector shrugs as if mortality were a scheduling problem. "I can't take
him like that. It's against regulations."

"What you are watching," he tells you, dropping the act for a moment, "is
convenience arguing with evidence. The plague makes everyone hurry. Hurry makes
people treat 'almost' as 'done'. The man is speaking. That is data. 'I'm
getting better' is also data, even if it is optimistic data."

> If someone can still argue with you, do not load them on the cart. Check the
> evidence before you declare the case closed.

"In a plague, and in a project, the same failure shows up: declaring something
finished because finishing would be tidy. Your door opens when you can tell
tidiness from truth."

- [ ] True
- [x] False

> A prediction is not a present fact. The sketch is funny because the relative
> wants the administrative category now, and the body has not agreed.

> The relative wants the cart to solve a problem. The Collector wants a clean
