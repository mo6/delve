# None shall pass

The Black Knight stands in the middle of the path and does not step aside. His
armour is black. His policy is shorter.

"None shall pass," he says.

Arthur explains that he is king, that he is on a quest, that this ford is not
worth dying over. The Knight is unmoved. The fight begins because one person
has turned a stretch of mud into a matter of absolute principle, and the other
person needs to get across.

"I am not here to teach you swordplay," the Knight continues, as if that were
in doubt. "I am here to teach you what a blocked path sounds like when someone
has confused *stubbornness* with *duty*. 'None shall pass' is clear. It is
also total. There is no exception for kings, quests, or people who only want
to use the ford for five minutes."

> Absolute refusals are easy to shout and expensive to keep. Ask what you are
> actually defending before you plant yourself in the road.

"Sometimes a line is worth holding. Sometimes you are guarding a puddle. The
examination is about telling the difference before the arms start coming off."

> When the rule is absolute, status and purpose are irrelevant. Reasoning only
> works if the other side can update. The Knight cannot, by design.

### Holding a hard line is always a sign of strong principles.

- [ ] True
- [x] False

> A hard line can mark a principle, or it can mark an ego that needs the road.
> Strength is judged by what the line protects, not by how loudly it is said.

### Which clarifying question would have been most useful before the fight started?

> Scope matters. "None shall pass" collapses every distinction. Asking what is
