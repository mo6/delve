# 'Tis but a scratch

Scratch has misplaced an arm, then another, then rather a lot of the rest. He
calls each loss a detail.

"'Tis but a scratch," he says. Then: "I've had worse." Then: "It's just a
flesh wound." Then, eventually, a claim that he will bite your legs off.

"Denial has a shape," he explains cheerfully. "First you minimise. Then you
redefine. Then you escalate the threat so nobody notices you have nothing left
to escalate with. The arms are on the ground. The argument has moved to the
mouth."

He nods at the floor. "In a fight, this is funny. In a decision, it is how
people bankrupt a project, a friendship, or a kingdom: by refusing to update
the story when the evidence is lying in the grass."

> When the facts have changed and your slogan has not, you are no longer
> defending a position. You are defending your refusal to notice.

"Knowing when you have lost is not cowardice. It is how you keep the legs you
still have. Come back here and I will explain further. Or don't. That is also
a kind of wisdom."
