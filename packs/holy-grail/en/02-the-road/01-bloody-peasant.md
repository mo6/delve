# Strange women lying in ponds

Dennis is covered in filth and entirely unimpressed by kings. Arthur explains
the Lady of the Lake, the sword, the mandate of heaven. Dennis would like a
word about waterlogged women and executive power.

"Look," he says, "you can't expect to wield supreme executive power just
because some watery tart threw a sword at you."

He digs another handful of mud. "Arthur arrives with a story about legitimacy.
I arrive with a question about consent. Who agreed to be governed by this
method? Was there an election? Or are we doing political theory by aquatic
projectile?"

"The scene is not anti-story. It is anti-*unexamined* story. 'I am king'
is a claim. 'Because a lady in a lake said so' is a reason that needs more
work before it binds anyone else. Mockery is how Dennis forces the claim into
the open."

> A founding story is not the same as consent. Ask who agreed, not only who
> holds the sword.

"You may still prefer Arthur to Dennis. Prefer him after you have heard the
objection."
