# African or European swallow?

Velocity has a small chart of birds and a large amount of patience for people
who skip the units.

"They ask what the airspeed velocity of an unladen swallow is," she says.
"And the only correct first reply is: African or European? Not because you are
showing off. Because the question, as asked, is underspecified. Different
birds, different builds, different answers. If you invent a single number
anyway, you are not being brave. You are guessing with confidence."

She taps the chart. "This is the Bridge of Death rule, and also the courtyard
rule. Clarifying questions are not pedantry when the answer hangs on the
distinction. 'Unladen' matters. Species matters. Whether the swallow is
carrying a coconut matters so much that the whole coconut debate exists."

"Arthur fails at the bridge because he cannot answer. The swallows who carried
coconuts, if they did, needed a grip and a plan. The comedy is ornithology as
mortal stakes."

> When a question hides a fork, name the fork before you answer. "African or
> European?" is not nitpicking; it is the work.

"Leave this floor able to say: I need one distinction before I pretend I know."
