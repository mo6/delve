# 'Tis a silly place

Camelot is mid-song: knights, kicking, dining, and a great deal of enthusiasm.
Arthur watches for one verse too many, then turns around.

"On second thought," he says, "let's not go to Camelot. 'Tis a silly place."

The Chorus looks briefly offended, then professionally cheerful again. "We are
the temptation to stay for the production number," they explain. "Questing is
hard. A castle with a song feels like arrival. Arthur's joke is that arrival
can be a trap: you came for a mission and nearly settled for a revue."

"God is about to hand him an actual assignment. Walking away from the silly
place is how he keeps capacity for the real one."

> Spectacle is not progress. If the place is silly, you are allowed to leave
> before the second chorus.

"Your examination is about recognising when entertainment is substituting for
the thing you said you came to do."

## Questions

### Arthur decides not to enter Camelot after the musical number. What judgment is he making?

- [ ] That singing is forbidden to knights

- [ ] That Camelot does not exist
- [ ] That God prefers French castles

> He names the place silly and leaves. The point is opportunity cost: the song
> is fun, the Grail is elsewhere.

### Staying for every impressive ceremony is always the loyal choice.

- [ ] True
- [x] False

> Loyalty to a mission sometimes means skipping the revue. The film treats
> walking away as wisdom, not rudeness.

### Which workplace failure is closest to getting stuck in Camelot?

- [ ] Shipping the work you promised
- [ ] Declining a meeting with no agenda
- [x] Attending every flashy kickoff and never doing the quest
- [ ] Writing down the actual goal

> Kickoffs and songs feel like motion. They are not the Grail. The other
> options are how you escape Camelot.

### The useful sentence to borrow from Arthur is:

- [ ] "I am invincible!"
- [x] "On second thought, let's not."
- [ ] "Ni!"
- [ ] "Bring out your dead!"

> Second thoughts are allowed. The door opens for people who can still redirect
> after the music starts.
