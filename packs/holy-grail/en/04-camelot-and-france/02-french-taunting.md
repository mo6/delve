# I fart in your general direction

The Taunter is on the wall, inventing insults faster than Arthur can invent
strategy. The Britons came for the Grail. They are receiving livestock.

"You empty-headed animal-food-trough wiper," he begins, and continues in that
vein until a cow leaves the battlements at speed.

"Taunting is a weapon," he confides. "Not because the words are true, but
because they pull you off your aim. Arthur wants the Grail. I want Arthur
angry, noisy, and standing where I can drop farmyard ordinance on him."

"If you answer every insult, you are playing my game. If you notice that the
castle's claim to hold the Grail is unverified, you are playing yours."

> Do not let mockery choose your next move. Check the claim; ignore the bait.

"Also, I am French. That is load-bearing for the joke. Do not relocate me to
Belgium in your head."

> The content is nonsense. The function is bait. While Arthur reacts, the
> strategic question (is the Grail even here?) goes untested.

### A barrage of farm animals is best read as:

- [ ] A gift of provisions
- [ ] Proof of cultural exchange
- [x] Escalation meant to end the conversation on their terms
- [ ] A traditional Grail-handover ritual

> Cows and chickens are punctuation. They close the talks without conceding
> anything.

### You should treat every insult as a claim that must be rebutted point by point.

- [ ] True
- [x] False

> Point-by-point rebuttal is how you lose an afternoon to someone who is not
> arguing in good faith. Separate bait from the decision you still need to make.

### Which response best keeps Arthur's actual goal in view?

- [ ] Inventing a longer insult
- [ ] Standing under the walls indefinitely
- [x] Testing whether the Grail claim is real, then choosing another route if it is not
- [ ] Asking the Taunter for his favourite colour

> Goal first. The taunts are weather. The unverified Grail claim is the plot.
